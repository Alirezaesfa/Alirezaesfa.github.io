Paradoxical Value Co-Creation Through Destructive Behaviors: Trash Talking in Dota 2

Co-creation is usually tied to positive behavior. My thesis asked how a negative behavior, trash talking in one of the most competitive online games, can still create value for the people involved.

How I did it

  • Recruited active players through in-game chat and the game's main forum, and ran 10 semi-structured interviews of 30 to 60 minutes over Zoom.
  • Archived 10 forum threads with 20+ comments each using a Python archiver, to add the wider community's view.
  • Coded the transcripts inductively (first-order codes, second-order themes, aggregate dimensions) and adapted the interview guide after the first five interviews as themes emerged.

What I found

Within limits, trash talking creates four kinds of value: competition (constructive criticism and psychological pressure), fun, self-image (feeling and playing better) and social bonding among friends. It stops creating value when it becomes personal, bigoted or aimed at demoralizing teammates.

Why it matters for products

The findings point to designing for the behavior instead of only banning it: structured ways to channel competitive banter, such as in-game communication tools, and treating harsh feedback as input to improve a product.
